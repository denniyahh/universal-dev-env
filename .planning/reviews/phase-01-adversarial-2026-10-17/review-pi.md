## Verdict

No — I would not let an automated executor run these 11 plans as written. Two of the failures are blocking and deterministic: 01-02's acceptance gate is unsatisfiable without breaking an unrelated doctor test (C1), and 01-06 mandates a `StagedTransaction` shape that cannot both cleanup on `Drop` and move its `NamedTempFile`s out of `commit(self)` without an instruction the plan never gives (C2); 01-07's own acceptance grep also fails by construction (C7). Beyond the mechanical failures, the deletion/conflict truth table is genuinely ambiguous for a state the phase's own flows create (C3), and the phase introduces a new un-locked writer of the single source of truth (C5/case C4) while asserting in a must_have that writes serialize. The plans are unusually well-instrumented — real negative controls, measured commands, explicit `[ASSUMED]` tags — but the instrumentation is concentrated on the happy paths; the defects that survive are exactly the ones where two plans each define their half of a shared state machine and neither owns the intersection. Fix C1, C2, C3, C4, C5 and C7 (plus the 01-07:164 and 01-07:190 criteria) before delegating; the rest can ride with the executor.
